## 🌍 1. Introduction (Renewable vs Non-Renewable)

**Renewable Resources kya hain?** Wo natural resources jo kabhi khatam nahi hote kyunki nature unhe naturally replace karti hai. Inki supply endless hoti hai. Jaise: solar energy, wind energy, hydro energy.

*   **Scientific Definition:** Agar kisi resource ka depletion/usage rate, uske replenishment rate se kam hai, toh wo Renewable Resource hai.
*   **Perpetual Resources:** Kuch resources (sun, air, wind, tides) ko perpetual kehte hain kyunki human consumption se unki quantity affect nahi hoti.
*   **Biotic Resources:** Living beings se milne wale resources (fish, animals, birds, mineral fuels jo decayed organic matter se bante hain).
*   **Abiotic Resources:** Non-living resources (land, air, water, ores).

**Non-Renewable Resources kya hain?** Ye wo resources hain jo lakhon saalon ke long geological processes se bante hain. Inka formation rate bahut slow hai, isliye ye consume hone ke same rate par replenish nahi ho sakte. Jaise: fossil fuels (coal, petroleum), minerals. Inhe **"Dirty Energy"** bhi kehte hain kyunki ye pollution aur environmental issues cause karte hain.

> [!NOTE]
> **PDF ki Study Notes:**
> *   **Kyoto Protocol:** Pehla major international accord tha jo CO2 emission kam karne aur global warming reduce karne ke liye banaya gaya.
> *   **IRENA Survey (2019):** Renewable resources se produce hone wali energy ki price fossil fuels ke comparable hai.
> *   **U.S. (2017):** 11% energy consumption renewable resources se aati hai.

**Renewable Resources ke Challenges:**
1.  **Less Reliable:** Seasonal changes production ko impact karte hain (jaise winter mein solar energy kam).
2.  **Not Always Perfectly Renewable:** Agar human consumption badh jaye toh production rate gir sakta hai.
3.  **Destruction:** Urban sprawl, irrigation, deforestation, grazing, fishing, hunting se renewable resources destroy ho sakte hain.
4.  **Costly:** Large scale use costly hai, aur research ki zaroorat hai.

**Government Incentives:**
*   **Energy Tax:** Fossil fuels par surcharge lagta hai, jisse renewable resources competitive ho jate hain.
*   **Green Fund:** Ek mutual fund jo sirf un companies mein invest karta hai jo environmentally responsible goods produce karti hain.

## 💧 2. Water Resources & Overexploitation

**Hydrologic Cycle (Water Cycle):** Ye ek natural cycle hai jo sunlight aur gravity se powered hai. Paani evaporate hota hai, vapor banta hai, atmosphere mein hold hota hai, aur phir precipitation (barish) ke roop mein neeche aata hai.

### Forms of Water (Paani 3 forms mein milta hai):
1.  **Surface Water:** Melted snow aur precipitation ka residue. Ise **Runoff** bhi kehte hain. Rivers, streams, lakes tab bante hain jab precipitation rate evaporation rate se zyada ho.
2.  **Groundwater:** Wo paani jo earth surface ke andar infiltrate karta hai. Ye porous rocks aur soils ke layers se neeche jata hai jinhe **Aquifers** kehte hain. Ye gravity ki wajah se neeche jata hai.
3.  **Frozen Water:** Cold regions mein glaciers aur ice ke roop mein.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Water Distribution:**
> *   97% paani saltwater hai (human consumption ke liye fit nahi).
> *   3% fresh water hai, jisme se bada hissa polar ice caps mein locked hai.
> *   Sirf **0.003%** fresh water surface aur groundwater ke roop mein readily available hai.

**Water ke Uses:** Drinking, Irrigation, Washing, Waste disposal by industries, Coolant for thermal power plants.

### 2.1 Overexploitation of Groundwater and Surface Water

Water resources ka overuse hone se paani scarce ho raha hai. Rivers, groundwater, streams sookh rahe hain. **Scarcity ke Reasons:**
*   **Drastic Changes in Rainfall Patterns:** Deforestation ki wajah se kam barish hoti hai aur groundwater recharge kam hota hai.
*   **Groundwater Mining:** Multinational companies (jaise bottled water aur soft drinks) groundwater ko overexploit karti hain aur effluents release karke pollute bhi karti hain.
*   **Subsidence:** Jab groundwater withdrawal rate recharge rate se zyada ho jata hai, toh aquifer ke sediments compact ho jate hain aur upar ki land surface sink ho jati hai. Isse buildings mein structural damages, pipe cracks, sewer flow reversal, tidal flooding jaise issues hote hain.
*   **Water Logging:** Excessive irrigation se water table rise hota hai. Jab rocks mein salt is paani ke contact mein aata hai, toh salt rise karke upper soil layer mein evaporate ho jata hai, jisse soil saline aur infertile ho jati hai.
*   **Floods:** Deforestation, rapid industrialization, mining, grazing, global warming se floods badh rahe hain. Global warming se zyada paani evaporate hota hai, jisse heavy rainfall hoti hai.
*   **Drought:** Deforestation, grazing, mining jaise anthropogenic causes se below normal rainfall hoti hai. Erroneous cropping patterns aur increased water exploitation bhi drought cause karte hain.

> [!NOTE]
> **PDF ki Study Note:** 2016 mein MoWRGR (Ministry of Water Resources) ki report ne conclude kiya ki water governance jaise complex issue ke liye multidisciplinary approach chahiye.

## ⚔️ 3. Conflicts Over Water

World population last century mein 3x badh gayi hai, isliye water resources par tremendous pressure hai. Water disputes local, district, inter-state, national aur international levels par badh rahe hain.

**International Water Disputes (Resolved):**
*   **1960 - India & Pakistan (Indus River System):** World Bank ki mediation se resolve hua. Indus River Treaty mein 6 rivers divide hui. Sutlej, Beas, Ravi India ko; Indus, Chenab, Jhelum Pakistan ko.
*   **1959 - Egypt & Sudan (Nile Waters):** Full utilization agreement ke saath resolve hua.

**Unresolved Disputes:**
*   Iraq, Turkey, Syria (Euphrates & Tigris).
*   Syria, Palestine, Israel, Lebanon (Jordan River).

**India ke 3 Main Stressors (Interstate Water Conflicts):**
1.  **Federal Structure:** Water state ka subject hai. Example: Cauvery water dispute (Karnataka vs Tamil Nadu). Law water source ke state ko primary right deta hai (upstream users).
2.  **Food Security:** Wheat aur rice jaise high water-consuming crops ko promote kiya gaya. Green Revolution (1960s) ne low water-consuming crops (ragi, sorghum) ko displace kar diya.
3.  **Water Policy:** Land, water, food ke nexus ki understanding missing hai. Integrated ecosystem approach nahi hai; piecemeal program hai.

## ⚡ 4. Renewable Energy Sources

Non-renewable sources environment aur human health ke liye hazardous hain. Inse global warming hota hai. Jaise **Fracking** (high pressure liquid rocks mein inject karke gas/oil extract karna) se water pollution aur earthquakes ho sakte hain.

**Common Types of Renewable Energy:**

1.  **Solar or Light Energy:** Sun ki radiation se **Photovoltaic cells** (silicon se bane) electricity produce karte hain. NREL kehta hai ki Earth par 1 hour mein girne wali solar energy, duniya ki 1 saal ki electricity use se zyada hai. Solar farms large-scale installations hain.
    *   *Note:* Large hydroelectric power plants (mega-dams) non-renewable category mein aate hain kyunki ye natural water flow ko restrict karte hain.
2.  **Wind Energy:** Wind turbines ke blades ghumne se mechanical energy electricity mein convert hoti hai. Ye energy power grid mein store bhi ho sakti hai.
3.  **Hydroelectric Power:** Fast-moving water (river ya high point se girta paani) se electricity. U.S. mein ye largest renewable energy resource hai (10% total electricity).
4.  **Biomass Energy:** Plants aur animals ke organic materials (wood, crops, ethanol, sewage). Biomass ne sun ki energy absorb ki hoti hai, jo burn karne par heat energy release hoti hai. Fossil fuels se kam carbon release karta hai.
5.  **Geothermal Power:** Earth ke core ki heat se. Core mein radioactive particles decay hote hain aur heat release karte hain. Deep wells bore karke hot water hydrothermal source ke roop mein bahar aata hai. Isse electricity ya heat generate hoti hai. High upfront cost ki wajah se adoption slow hai.
6.  **Tidal Energy:** Tides ki energy. Abhi developmental phase mein hai par potential enormous hai. Tidal turbines seafloor par lagaye jate hain jahan strong tidal flows hoti hain. Tidal stream generators ocean ki kinetic energy se power generate karte hain.

> [!NOTE]
> **Nuclear Fusion:** Light elements ke nuclear reactions se heavier elements bante hain aur substantial energy release hoti hai. Abhi tak perfected nahi hua hai.

## 🔋 5. Alternate Energy Sources & Growing Needs

**Alternative Energy kya hai?** Ye wo energy sources hain jo fossil fuels se derive nahi hote, isliye ye zero ya very little greenhouse gases produce karte hain. Inka main aim fossil fuels se bachna hai.

*   **Difference from Renewable:** Renewable resources nature mein abundant hain aur constantly replenish hote hain. Alternative energy (jaise nuclear power) thorium ya uranium use karti hai, jo finite resources hain. Solar power dono renewable aur alternative hai.

**Nuclear Energy:** Atom ke core se energy, **fission** (splitting) process se. Controlled nuclear fission reactors mein hoti hai. Ye alternative hai par renewable nahi kyunki uranium finite hai.

### 4.1 Growing Energy Needs
IEA (International Energy Agency) ke projections:
*   2005-2030 ke beech energy needs mein **55% increase**.
*   Oil demand 11.7 billion tons se 17.7 billion tons tak.
*   Energy consumption mein **50% increase**.
*   Zyada demand developing countries se aayegi (India aur China lead kar rahe hain).
*   **Reasons:** Population growth, wealth increase, technological developments, better standard of living.
*   Aaj duniya ki **26% electricity** renewable sources se aati hai; IEA ke according 2024 tak ye **30%** ho jayegi.

> [!NOTE]
> **UK Milestone (2020):** UK ne 10 June 2020 ko 2 months tak sirf renewable energy sources par run kiya. UK energy produce aur consume karne mein efficient hai.

## ⛏️ 6. Minerals, Metals & Non-Renewable Energy

Minerals, metal ores, fossil fuels aur groundwater non-renewable resources ke examples hain. Metals Earth's crust mein abundant hain, par extraction wahan hota hai jahan natural geological processes (heat, pressure, organic activity) se concentrated hote hain. Ye formation millions of years leta hai.

### 5.1 Minerals
Minerals wo hain jinse metals extract hote hain. **Examples:** Diamonds, gypsum, limestone, potash, salt, sulphur, peat.
*   **Asbestos:** Minerals ka group, fibres ke bundling ke roop mein. Commercial/industrial purposes (automotive breaks, wallboard materials) mein use hota hai. Kuch asbestos lung diseases cause karte hain.
*   **Limestone:** Carbonated sedimentary rock, cement aur plaster banane mein use hota hai.
*   **Potash:** Potassium-containing fertilizers ke liye.
*   **Salt (Sodium Chloride):** Food additive aur flavouring.
*   **Sulphur:** Hydrogen sulphide se, chemical manufacturing mein.
*   **Aggregates:** Sand, gravel, crushed stones construction industry mein.

### 5.2 Metals
Metals ke beneficial physical aur chemical properties hain. Basic form, alloys aur compounds mein use hote hain.
*   **Practical Uses:** Equipment, machines, electricity-conducting wires, buildings.
*   **Important Metals:** Aluminium (Al), Chromium (Cr), Cobalt (Co), Copper (Cu), Iron (Fe), Lead (Pb), Manganese (Mn), Mercury (Hg), Nickel (Ni), Tin (Sn), Uranium (U), Zinc (Zn).
*   **Precious Metals:** Gold (Au), Platinum (Pt), Silver (Ag) — jewellery aur electronics conductors.
*   **Alloys:** Brass (Cu + Zn), Bronze (Cu + Sn + Zn/Pb), Steel (Fe + C + Cr/Mn/Ni).
*   **Tailings:** Target mineral extract karne ke baad bacha waste material, jo landfill/lake/ocean mein dump hota hai.

### 5.3 Other Non-Renewable Energy Sources

#### 🪨 Coal
Solid fossil fuel, carbon-heavy rock, decayed plants/animals se bana. Most carbon-intensive fossil fuel.
*   **India:** 2nd largest coal-producing country (729 million tonnes, Dec 2020). States: Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal, Chhattisgarh.
*   **Types (4):**
    1.  **Anthracite:** Carbon 86-97%, highest heating value, high-quality. India mein J&K.
    2.  **Bituminous:** Carbon 45-86%, electricity, iron & steel industry. India: WB, Jharkhand, Odisha, MP.
    3.  **Sub-bituminous:** Carbon 35-45%, lower heating than bituminous. U.S.: Wyoming, Montana.
    4.  **Lignite:** Carbon 25-35%, lowest energy, young deposits, high moisture. India: Rajasthan, Assam, Tamil Nadu.
*   **Extraction Methods:** Underground mining (heavy machinery), Surface/Strip mining (U.S. mein common, most catastrophic & polluting).
*   **India Facts:** Coal mining 1774 se shuru. Dhanbad largest coal-producing city. India mein coal-derived energy oil-derived energy se zyada hai (world mein ulta hai).

#### 🛢️ Oil
Liquid fossil fuel. **Crude oil** volatile liquid hydrocarbons + nitrogen, sulphur, oxygen.
*   **Extraction:** Underground reservoirs, cracks/crevices/pores of sedimentary rock, tar sands.
*   **Methods:** Drilling on land/sea, Strip mining of tar sands/oil shale.
*   **Products:** Gasoline, kerosene, jet fuel, plastics, paint.
*   **India:** New oil discoveries ki scarcity, COVID-19 se 7.1% fall (May 2020), imports par dependence badhi.

#### 💨 Natural Gas
Fossil gas, hydrocarbon gas mixture — mainly methane + higher alkanes, CO2, nitrogen, H2S, helium.
*   **Formation:** Millions of years, decomposing plants/animals intense temperature ke under.
*   **Uses:** Heating, cooking, electricity generation, vehicle fuel, plastics/organic chemicals (chemical feedstock).
*   **Types:**
    *   **Conventional:** Porous/permeable rocks ya oil reservoirs mein mixed, standard drilling se extracted.
    *   **Unconventional:** Long process, special stimulation techniques (fracking) se extracted.

#### ☢️ Uranium
Nuclear fuel, Uranium ore se extracted. Radioactive element, naturally below earth's surface/sea water mein. Nuclear plants **nuclear fission** ke liye use karte hain.
*   **U-235:** Fuel ke liye use hota hai kyunki atoms easily split hote hain. Uranium silver se zyada common hai par U-235 relatively rare.
*   **Uranium Fuel Cycle:** Mining → Milling (yellowcake) → Conversion to UF6 gas → Enrichment (U-235 increase) → Fuel pellets → Electricity → Used fuel storage/reprocessing.
*   **Uses:** Nuclear power plants fuel, high-density penetrators, shielding material, aircraft counterweights, missile ballast.
*   **Features:** Symbol U, atomic number 92, silver-grey metal.

#### ⚛️ Thorium
Weak radioactive metallic element, symbol Th, atomic number 90. Silver, tarnishes black (thorium dioxide), hard, malleable, high melting point, reactive.
*   **Advantages over Uranium:** Less radioactive waste, better chemical stability, more common than uranium.
*   **Downsides:** Directly reactor power nahi kar sakta (brittle material insufficient), industry lacks operational experience, fuel preparation cumbersome, 550°C more to melt than uranium dioxide, immobile (chemical processing difficult), irradiated thorium short-term more radioactive/hazardous, gamma rays hard to block, commercial scale efficiency unproven.

## ⚠️ 7. Use & Overuse of Non-Renewable Resources

**Overconsumption:** Industrial revolution + technology + research ne mineral/oil exploration easy banaya. Increased exploitation se oil, gas, mineral depletion hui. Minerals (Gasoline, Copper, Zinc) production next 20 years mein reduce hogi. Toxins aur chemical by-products release badh rahe hain, jo acidic lakes, dead zones, wildlife death cause karte hain.

**Factors Steering Overuse:**
1.  **Technological Advancement:** Quick & efficient extraction, deforestation by urbanisation.
2.  **Increasing Population:** UN reports — 7.8 billion (2020) → 9.9 billion (2050 expected).
3.  **Consumerism Culture:** Increased production & consumption, overuse & depletion.
4.  **Materialistic Views:** Necessary aur unnecessary products excess mein.
5.  **Research & Effort:** Extraction rate, availability, consistent supply badhi.
6.  **Efficiency:** Minimal fuel se lot of energy.
7.  **Ease of Transportation:** Underground pipes for oil & gas.
8.  **Easy Set-up:** Fuel plant kahin bhi set up ho sakta hai.

### Resource Depletion
Jab consumption replenishment se aage nikal jaye. Isse resource ki value badhti hai.
**Common Types:** Aquifer depletion, Deforestation, Mining for fossil fuels/minerals, Pollution/contamination, Slash-and-burn agriculture, Soil erosion, Overconsumption.

*   **Peak Oil:** Maximum global petroleum extraction ke baad production decline.
*   **Water:** UN forecast — 2025 tak 1.8 billion people drinking water scarcity face karenge. Groundwater availability 22.6 million cubic km, renewable portion only 0.35 million. Renewal ~50 years. Extraction 1960 se doubled.
*   **Deforestation:** Population growth farming, agriculture, housing badhata hai. Half of forest depleted.
*   **Coal:** Maximum extraction 2025-2048 expected. Sufficient coal for 188 years (agar demand fast badhi toh kam hoga).
*   **Phosphorous:** Phosphorous rock & guano se. Fertilizers mein use. Global peak production ~2030 (Global Phosphorous Research Initiative).

## 📖 8. Glossary (Important Terms)

*   **Anthropogenic:** Human activities jo pollution aur environmental degradation cause karti hain.
*   **Effluents:** Waste sewage (water & gas) ka natural water bodies (rivers, sea) mein discharge.
*   **Environmental Economists:** Wo log jo natural resources ki economics study karte hain (extraction, use, economic incentives for environment).
*   **Marshes:** Wetland jahan herbaceous plants dominate karti hain (river/stream/lake edges, terrestrial to aquatic transition).
*   **Subsistence Farming:** Farmers apne use ke liye crops/livestock grow karte hain (no surplus for trade).
*   **Solar Farms:** Large-scale solar panel installations (solar parks), power plants ki tarah operate.
*   **Terrestrial:** Land se related cheezein (forests, deserts, grasslands).
*   **By-products:** Production process mein incidentally produce hone wala product.
*   **Biomass:** Plant/animal substance jo heat/electricity generate kar sakta hai.
*   **Contamination:** Resources mein undesired substance ki presence, jisse wo unfit ho jaye.
*   **Fertiliser:** Natural/synthetic material jo soil & plants ko essential nutrients de.
*   **Greenhouse Effect:** Earth atmosphere ka temperature badhna, jab greenhouse gases surface se emitted heat ko attract karti hain.
*   **Greenhouse Gas:** Gas jo infrared radiation absorb, store, reradiate karti hai.
*   **Nuclear Fission:** Nuclear reaction jisme atom ka nucleus multiple lighter nuclei mein split hota hai.
*   **Oil Spills:** Liquid petroleum hydrocarbon ka earth surface par leakage.
*   **Ore:** Naturally available substance (rock/sediments) jisse minerals/metals extract hote hain.
*   **Recycling:** Unwanted material ko useful products mein convert karna.
*   **Renewable Energy:** Nature se energy jo human timescale par naturally replenish hoti hai.
*   **Sustainability:** Resources ko incessantly provide & retain karne ki ability.
*   **Solar Power:** Sunlight ko electricity mein convert karna.
*   **Urbanisation:** Process jisse urban area badhta hai aur rural area kam hota hai.

## ☕ 9. Case Study: Starbucks

**Background:** 21 January 2020 ko Starbucks ne announce kiya ki wo next decade mein greenhouse gases aur waste ko half karega. 2030 tak business operations aur coffee production mein use hone wale **50% water** ko replenish/conserve karega.

**Strategies (Getting Greener):**
*   Reusing packaging, healthier cuisine menu.
*   2021 (50th anniversary) tak environmental goals par more data disclose.
*   U.S. & Canada operations ko power karne ke liye enough renewable energy purchase kiya.
*   **Setback:** 2008 mein reusable containers mein 25% drinks serve karne ka target tha, 2015 tak 5% kar diya, 2018 tak sirf 1.3% serve kar paye.

**Environmental Footprint:**
*   GHG emission ~14 coal power plants ke pollution ke equivalent (Microsoft jaise giants ke barabar).
*   Water consumption 4 lakh Olympic-sized swimming pools ke barabar.
*   80% carbon footprint stores mein energy consumption se.

**Energy Saving Initiatives:**
*   Retail outlets mein energy consumption 25% reduce karna.
*   7,000+ stores mein LED lights (incandescent/halogen replace), 80% energy reduce.
*   DOE reports: LED se $30 annual savings (1,000 sq ft store: $600/year, 10 barrels oil CO2 elimination).
*   2016: Energy Management System implemented.
*   Suppliers ko energy-saving equipment ke liye push kiya.
*   **Challenge:** Menu changes (oven/refrigerator usage) se savings offset.

**Renewable Energy:**
*   EPA's Green Power List — coffee industry mein pioneer organic electricity buyers.
*   Green-E certified Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) procure karna.
*   **Greener Retail:** Verification program, 10,000 new stores by 2025.
*   **Sustainable Coffee Challenge:** 100% sustainable coffee, profitable production, sustainable agriculture, deforestation restriction.

**Discussion Questions:**
1.  Starbucks ne environment protect karne ke liye right step liya? 'Green Retail' se kya help milegi?
2.  Companies non-renewable resources consumption kaise kam kar sakti hain? Substitutes kya hain?

## 📝 10. Quick Revision (Terminal Q&A)

**Q1. Define "renewable" in reference to natural resources.**
*   *Ans:* Renewable resources wo hain jo naturally replace ho jate hain aur kabhi khatam nahi hote. Inka consumption rate replenishment rate se kam hota hai (e.g., solar, wind, hydro).

**Q2. Give a brief introduction to minerals and coals.**
*   *Ans:* Minerals wo non-renewable resources hain jinse metals extract hote hain (diamonds, gypsum, limestone, etc.). Coal ek solid fossil fuel hai, carbon-heavy rock, decayed plants/animals se bana. Ye most carbon-intensive fossil fuel hai.

**Q3. Briefly explain major fossil fuels utilised for energy generation.**
*   *Ans:* Coal (37% global electricity demand), Natural Gas (heating, cooking, electricity, plastics), Petroleum/Oil (gasoline, diesel, jet fuel, petrochemicals).

**Q4. List out the triggers that entail the usage of non-renewable energy resources.**
*   *Ans:* Population increase, technological innovations, industrialisation, urbanisation, urban development.

**Q5. Write a short note on non-renewable natural resources.**
*   *Ans:* Ye wo resources hain jo normal timescale mein replenish nahi ho sakte. Inka formation millions of years leta hai. Main types: coal, oil, natural gas. Ye country ki principal energy form hain.

**Q6. What are the three main water source types?**
*   *Ans:* Surface water (runoff, rivers/lakes), Groundwater (aquifers mein infiltrated water), Frozen water (glaciers/ice).

**Q7. Explain any three adverse effects of overexploitation of water resources.**
*   *Ans:* Groundwater mining (companies overexploit & pollute), Subsidence (land surface sinking due to compaction), Water logging (excessive irrigation se salinity & soil infertility).

**Q8. Explain any four types of renewable energy sources.**
*   *Ans:* Solar (photovoltaic cells), Wind (turbines), Hydroelectric (fast-moving water), Biomass (organic materials burn karke heat energy).
